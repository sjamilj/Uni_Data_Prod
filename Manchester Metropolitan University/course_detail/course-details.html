Course overview

How do we think and interact with the world? Why do we react in the ways we do? Explore the ways our minds work.

Our course covers all core elements of psychological study, from lifespan development, neuropsychology, and research methods to social, critical and cognitive psychology. 

You’ll learn about a wide range of methods used in psychology research. During your studies, you might be conducting experiments, developing an independent research proposal, and learning about qualitative methodologies. 

We support our students to develop their natural curiosity, creating psychology graduates who are equipped to explore the human experience in an ethical, empathetic, and scientific manner. 

Some of the topics you’ll study include the influence of biology and genetics on behaviour, perception and memory, mental health and cultural differences, and the importance of ethical practice.

Course information

You’ll have lots of variety each week, as you’ll experience different teaching methods across each psychology topic. You’ll usually have days with a mix of lectures, practical workshops, and group work.

Assessment is through coursework, which may include essays, reports, case studies, and individual and group presentations. 

In your final year of study, you’ll be able to choose option modules in areas that you’re particularly interested in, with themes such as educational psychology, health psychology, mental health, and human performance. You’ll be based in our award-winning Brooks building, which houses outstanding specialist facilities across all areas of health, psychology, social care, and education. We have six psychology laboratories and 12 experimental testing laboratories.  

Features and benefits

  • Teaching expertise - You’re taught by a large and diverse teaching team, with specialist lecturers who cover the whole field of psychology, such as forensic, counselling, clinical, health, and neuroscience. 
  • Enhanced student support - We strongly believe in enhancing student wellbeing. You’ll have a personal tutor and the programme support tutors will also work closely with you throughout the course. 

  • Outstanding facilities - YYou’ll have access to our specialist facilities for psychology demonstration and practice, which includes EEG and fNIRS for brain imaging, and TMS and tDCS for non-invasive brain stimulation. In addition, you will have access to experimental testing laboratories, including software for running and analysing studies in cognitive neuroscience, and systems for eye-tracking and for measuring physiological responses.

  • Rise programme - Go beyond your curriculum and engage with Rise and its unique skills-based workshops and projects. Gain additional credits and enhance your CV. 

  • Study Abroad Year - Immerse yourself in a vibrant culture at one of our partner universities in Spain, Italy, Sweden, Malaysia, USA, Canada or Australia. This opportunity is available after the completion of second year and gives you a global perspective on psychology. Our study abroad scheme offers unparalleled opportunities for personal growth, professional development and lifelong memories.  

  • Placement Year- The placement year, available after Year Two, offers the chance to gain hands-on work experience in a professional setting, and apply the knowledge gained during your degree to real-world work situations. It not only gives you the opportunity to develop your transferable skills, network and learn about how psychology applies in various settings, but it will also help you to stand out to potential employers in future job applications.

Year 1

In the first year, you will be introduced to the foundational concepts of psychological theory and research, exploring how these ideas shape our understanding of human behaviour in the context of today's world. With staff support and guidance, you will develop essential academic and professional skills that not only enhance your psychological knowledge but also help you begin to shape your own professional identity in the field.  

Core modules

Contemporary Psychology

Within this module you will consider psychological perspectives on hot topics and current societal challenges. You will learn the applied and real-world applications of psychological research and theory and consider how psychological science can contribute to improvements in practice, policy, performance and wellbeing.

Core Skills in Psychology

Within this module you will begin to understand and develop skills and knowledge that will boost your professionalism and enable career readiness through consideration of topics such as self-care and ethical practice, communication etiquette, IT literacy, professional identity, and professional boundaries. Practical applied activities will allow you to role play dilemmas and activities in various professional psychology contexts and reflect on your personal and professional development.

Psychology in Action

Within this module, you will be introduced to core and applied areas of psychology such as developmental, cognitive, biological, educational, health, forensic, and clinical. You will discover the breadth of psychology and uncover its role in understanding and shaping society.

Research Methods in Psychology 1

Through studying this module, you will gain key insights into the importance of good quality research in psychology. You will consider what research is, how it is conducted and learn basic methods of quantitative and qualitative analysis. You will carry out research studies and understand how to communicate your research findings according to academic conventions.

Year 2

Building on the knowledge from Year One, in Year Two you will dive deeper into key areas of psychology, taking a more critical and analytical approach to core topics in the field. This year focuses on an in-depth exploration of the British Psychological Society (BPS) curriculum, providing you with a robust understanding of psychology’s core principles. Additionally, at the end of Year Two, you’ll have the exciting option to enhance your learning with a sandwich placement year or a study abroad experience before returning to complete your final year.

Core modules

Cognitive and Biological Psychology

In this module you will critically explore key areas of cognitive psychological theory and research including discussion and evaluation of the theory and methodological issues. You will learn core aspects of cognitive psychology such as attention, perception, learning and memory, and the biological bases of human and non-human animal behaviour, such brain structure and function, hormones and behaviour, behavioural genetics, neuroscience, psychopharmacology, neuropsychology, comparative and evolutionary psychology.

Social and Developmental Psychology

In this module you will examine theoretical, methodological, and applied aspects of social and developmental psychology across the lifespan. You will study key aspects of developmental psychology including attachment, social relations, cognitive and language development, cognitive decline, biological aging, and cultural development. You will also study how social processes and situations affect behaviour, through processes such as attribution, attitudes, and group processes and intergroup relations.

Individuality and Diversity in Psychology

In this module you will study the social, conceptual, historical and cultural construction of psychology including the dominance and impact of privileged positions within psychology. You will gain key insights into how these perspectives shape understanding of individuality and diversity in terms of diversity in personality, intelligence, cognitive style, emotion, motivation, mood, physical and mental health (including social, biological and cognitive processes), as well as positive psychology.

Research Methods in Psychology 2

On this module you will build on your research skills from year one through designing ethical quantitative and qualitative research, recruiting participants, collecting data, and applying appropriate analysis techniques to address research questions. You will develop your academic writing skills through communicating research findings in practical report formats.

Year 3

In the final year, you’ll have the opportunity to specialise in areas of psychology that align with your personal interests and career goals. You will undertake an empirical research project in your chosen area of psychology, supported by a dissertation supervisor. You will also have the chance to tailor your studies further by selecting from a range of option modules, giving you the freedom to focus on the topics that inspire you most. 

Core modules

Psychology Futures

This module will introduce you to the practitioner psychology roles such as clinical psychologist, health psychologist, counselling psychologist, and forensic psychologist. You will consider how these roles help us to understand, support and change behaviour and wellbeing in a range of settings. wellbeing in a range of settings. Through reflective practice and application of theory and research, you’ll critically reflect on skills development in preparation for moving into the professional world. You’ll also explore how psychological skills and knowledge make a difference in settings and roles outside of psychology practice.

Preparing for Your Dissertation

In this module you will take ownership of the research development process through designing a feasible piece of applied psychology research that meets with Faculty and British Psychological Society guidelines and Code of Ethics. You will develop key competencies including self-efficacy as you work within a given time, logistical and ethical boundaries to develop a research question, building a rationale, designing a data collection strategy, and planning analysis for a research project that addresses a psychological challenge in contemporary society.

Dissertation

In this module you will work independently to conduct a piece of timely, ethical and meaningful psychological empirical research. You will develop self-efficacy as you work reflectively within a given timeframe, logistical and ethical boundaries to deliver an empirical research project that addresses a key psychological challenge in contemporary society. You will collect data, analyse data appropriately, and report the findings of your research in a professional academic format.

Study and assessment

Study

  • Year 1: 25% lectures, seminars or similar; 75% independent study
  • Year 2: 25% lectures, seminars or similar; 75% independent study
  • Year 3: 20% lectures, seminars or similar; 80% independent study

Assessment

  • Year 1: 100% coursework
  • Year 2: 100% coursework
  • Year 3: 100% coursework

Ten credits equates to 100 hours of study, which is a combination of lectures, seminars and practical sessions, and independent study. A three-year degree qualification typically comprises of 360 credits (120 credits per year). During the course, you are likely to be required to engage in collaborative projects, practical assessments, group work activities, and to give presentations to your peers/tutors. The exact composition of your study time and assessments for the course will vary according to your option choices and style of learning.

A career as unique as you

As a psychology graduate, there are a diverse range of career paths you could take. You might decide to pursue further training and a career as a psychologist or use your transferable skills to work across sectors from education, sport, public services, policing, mental health, to human resources and human performance. 

Depending upon the field of psychology you choose and the level of further training you engage in, some of the places you might work in are local authorities, social services, prisons, sports clubs, NHS or private hospitals, schools, or rehabilitation centres. 

Furthermore, we offer an exciting portfolio of Postgraduate programmes that will strengthen your specialist knowledge and skills and future prospects. 

Where do some of our graduates work? In housing services at Manchester City Council, as a probation case manager for Cheshire & Greater Manchester Community Rehabilitation Company, as a psychology assistant for an Early Intervention in Psychosis Team at an NHS Trust, and as a trainee High-Intensity CBT Therapist.
